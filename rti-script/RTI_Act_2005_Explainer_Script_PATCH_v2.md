## 2. Segment T0 — The legislative timeline

**Insert:** into `P01`, immediately after the "what is this Act" hook, before the preamble walk-through.
**Estimated runtime:** ~55 seconds.

### Narration

> Is Act ka janam-din note kar lijiye, kyunki exam mein dates poochhi jaati hain — aur ek date
> mein ek chhota sa trap bhi hai.
>
> Bill Lok Sabha mein pesh hua **23 December 2004** ko. Lok Sabha ne use paas kiya **11 May 2005**
> ko, aur Rajya Sabha ne **agle hi din** — **12 May 2005** ko. Presidential assent mila **15 June
> 2005** ko, President **Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam** se. Act statute book par aaya **Act No. 22 of
> 2005** bankar, aur Gazette of India mein chhapa **21 June 2005** ko.
>
> Lekin poori law lagu hui **12 October 2005** ko. Yeh Act ke poori tarah commence hone ka din hai.
>
> Aur ab trap. Aap padhenge ki yeh "enactment ke **120ve din**" hua. Arithmetically dekhein toh 15
> June se 12 October tak **119 din** hote hain. "120van din" tabhi banta hai jab aap **15 June ko
> hi pehla din ginein**. Dono baatein sahi hain — bas counting inclusive hai. Isliye safe line yeh
> hai: *"12 October 2005 — counting the date of enactment as day one, the 120th day."*
>
> Aur ek aur precision, jo zyada log miss karte hain: **Section 1(3) khud 120 din nahi bolta.**
> Section 1(3) kehta hai ki Act us date ko lagu hoga jo **Central Government notification se
> appoint kare**, aur proviso yeh bhi allow karta hai ki **alag-alag provisions ke liye alag-alag
> dates** ho sakti hain. 12 October ki date usi power ke tehat aayi. Act ke andar jo "120 din"
> likha hai, woh Section 4(1) mein hai — proactive disclosure ke liye — aur Section 5(1) mein **100
> din** hain PIO designate karne ke liye. In teen ko mix mat kijiye.
>
> Ab woh partial commencement. Kuch sections **15 June 2005 ko hi turant** lagu ho gaye:
> **4(1), 5(1), 5(2), 12, 13, 15, 16, 24, 27 aur 28**. Logic samajhiye — pehle machinery khadi
> karo. PIO aur APIO appoint ho, Central aur State Information Commissions banein, rules banne ki
> taqat mile, exemptions ka framework set ho. **Baaki sab — jismein woh section bhi hai jisse
> citizen ko information maangne ka substantive right milta hai — 12 October 2005 ko aaya.**
>
> Yaani teen mahine tak India mein ek aisa transparency law statute book par tha jiske tehat
> information maangi hi nahi ja sakti thi. Yeh detail chhoti lagti hai, lekin yeh batati hai ki
> law ka design kya tha: pehle institution, phir right.

### ADD-33 — Maharashtra RTI Rules, 2026: dilution by rulebook
**Priority: P0** · **Runtime: ~2 min 10 s** · **Insert:** new block; cross-ref from `ADD-1`, `ADD-20`, `BONUS-12`.

> Ab tak hum amendment ki baat kar rahe the — Parliament level par, sabke saamne. Lekin 2026 ne
> dikhaya ki dilution ka zyada asardaar raasta **rules** hain.
>
> **12 June 2026** ko Maharashtra ke General Administration Department ne **Maharashtra Right to
> Information Rules, 2026** notify kiye — Section 27 ke under, jo states ko procedure aur fees
> par rules banane ki taqat deta hai. Rules **publication ke turant baad** lagu ho gaye.
>
> Kya badla? Application fee **₹10 se ₹30**. Photocopy **₹2 se ₹5** per A4 page. Digital ya scanned
> copies, jo pehle free thi, ab **₹5 per page**. Inspection pehle ghante free, uske baad **₹50 per
> ghanta**. First appeal par **₹50**, second appeal par **₹100**. BPL applicants ke liye ab sirf
> **pehle 50 pages** free.
>
> Aur fees se aage — jo zyada khatarnaak tha: har application ke saath **photo ID / proof of
> citizenship**, application **150 shabdon** tak, **"ek subject, ek application"**, aur applicant ko
> **information maangne ka maksad** batana padta. Repeat applications ko summarily band karne ki
> taqat. Commission ki hearing mein **legal representation par rok**. Applicant absent ho toh appeal
> **dismiss**.
>
> Yahan RTI Act ka core principle yaad rakhiye: **Section 6(2) kehta hai ki applicant ko koi reason
> dene ki zaroorat nahi, aur koi personal detail dene ki zaroorat nahi** — bas woh information
> chahiye jo Act ke under aati ho. "Maksad batao" aur "ID dikhao" — dono iske seedha ulte hain.
>
> Anna Hazare ne **5 July se indefinite hunger strike** ki dhamki di. Activists, civil society aur
> legal experts ne rules par objection kiya. Aur **2 July 2026** ko Chief Minister Devendra Fadnavis
> ne rules par **stay** ka direction diya — order State Information Commission ke secretary Raviraj
> Falle ne jaari kiya.
>
> Uske baad, **7 July 2026** tak application fee **wapas ₹10** par aa gayi.
>
> Lekin ek baat clear kar dijiye, kyunki bahut se summaries yahan galat hain: Maharashtra mein appeal
> fee **₹20** hai — aur yeh **koi nayi cheez nahi hai**. ₹20 per appeal Maharashtra ka **purana,
> pre-2026** rate tha. Yaani rules ke jaane ke baad Maharashtra wapas usi position par aa gaya jahan
> woh pehle tha: **₹10 application, ₹20 appeal.**
>
> Is episode se teen lessons hain. **Ek** — dilution ke liye Act badalna zaroori nahi; Section 27 ka
> rulebook kaafi hai, aur rulebook par utni nazar nahi rehti. **Do** — ek state ka rulebook
> nationwide precedent ban sakta hai, isliye state notifications ko track karna national advocacy ka
> hissa hai. **Teen** — aur yeh sabse practical hai — **fee, word-limit aur ID ki demand
> Section 19 ke tehat appealable grounds hain.** Agar koi department bina statutory basis ke ₹10 se
> zyada maange, ya word limit par reject kare, toh woh refusal appeal mein challenge hota hai.

### ADD-34 — "Micro-dilutions": the vocabulary for invisible obstruction
**Priority: P2** · **Runtime: ~1 min 40 s** · **Insert:** new block, immediately after `ADD-33`.

> Maharashtra ek dramatic case tha — isliye woh news mein aaya. Lekin roz ka resistance drama nahi
> hota. Uske liye ek naam hai: **micro-dilutions**.
>
> Yeh framework February 2026 mein **NLS Forum** (National Law School of India University, Bengaluru
> ke Social Accountability Project) par chhapa. Iska evidence base thoda unusual hai — researcher ne
> khud **26 states mein 50 RTIs** file karke primary procedural data banaya, aur pichhle chaar saal
> mein **400 se zyada RTIs** file ki hain.
>
> Unhone jo pattern document kiya, woh yeh hai:
>
> **Ek — arbitrary scope aur word limits.** RTI Act mein application ki lambai par koi limit nahi
> hai. Phir bhi states aur authorities limits lagate hain. Bihar mein **2009 se 150-word limit**
> enforce ho rahi hai — ek applicant ki 10 mein se sirf 2 questions ka jawab mila, baaki "word limit
> ke bahar" the. Ek aur state mein 2006 ki notification ke tehat 50 pages se zyada wali information
> ke liye applicant ko office aana padta hai — jo door rehne wale ya kam mobility wale applicants ke
> liye practically denial hai.
>
> **Do — ID proof ki demand.** Haryana mein ek **2021 amendment** ne ID proof mandatory kiya; State
> Information Commission ne pehle uske khilaaf ruling di, phir use **uphold** kar diya — Section 27
> aur ek 2012 ke Punjab and Haryana High Court judgment ka hawala dete hue. Yaani ek procedural
> barrier ko **legalised** kar diya gaya, jab tak koi higher court use nahi hatata.
>
> **Teen — intent probing.** Applicant se poocha jaana ki "aapko yeh information kyun chahiye" aur
> "yeh public interest mein kaise hai" — jabki Act aur uske baad ke decisions saaf kehte hain ki
> applicant ko intent disclose karne ki zaroorat nahi.
>
> Aur ek reference point jo bahut kaam aata hai: **2012 ki Union guidelines** kehti hain ki RTI
> queries "ordinarily" 500 shabdon ke andar honi chahiye, **lekin koi application sirf isliye reject
> nahi ki ja sakti ki usmein 500 se zyada shabd hain.** "Ordinarily" ek guidance hai, ceiling nahi.
>
> Toh micro-dilution ka idea yeh hai: har ek rukawat chhoti lagti hai — ek form field, ek stamp, ek
> chhota sa charge, ek "office aa jaiye". Koi ek bhi akeli Act ka ullanghan nahi lagti. Lekin
> **cumulatively** yeh ek aisa filter bana dete hain jismein se aam citizen nikal hi nahi pata.
> Yeh amendment se zyada effective hai kyunki ispar headline nahi banti.
>
> Listener ke liye takeaway: jab bhi koi authority aapse **ID maange, maksad poochhe, word count
> gine, ya payment ka aisa raasta bataye jo practically possible na ho** — samajh lijiye ki aap
> ek micro-dilution ke saamne khade hain. Aur yeh sab **Section 19** ke tehat challengeable hain.

**Data card**

```
MICRO-DILUTIONS — NLS Forum, Feb 2026 (doi.org/10.55496/KDPT2827)
  Evidence   50 RTIs across 26 states + 400+ RTIs over 4 years
  Tactics    (1) word/question caps, "voluminous" labels
             (2) compulsory identity proof
             (3) probing the applicant's intent
             (4) payment/inspection hurdles that force abandonment
  Examples   Bihar — 150-word limit since 2009
             a 2006 state notification — >50 pages means visit in person
             Haryana — 2021 ID-proof amendment, later upheld by the SIC
                        (s.27 + a 2012 Punjab & Haryana HC judgment)
  Benchmark  2012 Union guidelines — "ordinarily" 500 words, and
             NO application may be rejected merely for exceeding it
```

### ADD-35 — Tamil Nadu, September 2026: Section 24(4) used, and reversed in six days
**Priority: P1** · **Runtime: ~1 min 50 s** · **Insert:** new block; cross-ref from `P10`, `P14`, `ADD-8`, `BONUS-14`.

> Section 24 ke baare mein humne padha ki state governments ko bhi taqat hai — **Section 24(4)** —
> apne dwara sthapit kisi intelligence ya security organisation ko exempt karne ki. Yeh provision
> kitni aasani se use ho sakti hai, iska live example September 2026 mein mila.
>
> **21 September 2026** ko Tamil Nadu ke Human Resources Management (R) Department ne **GO Ms. No.
> 57** jaari kiya. Usmein **Public (Law and Order) Department** ko **"Intelligence and Security
> Organisation"** declare karke Section 24(4) ke under RTI Act se exempt kar diya gaya. Gazette mein
> chhapa.
>
> Dhyan dijiye — exemption kisi intelligence agency ko nahi di gayi. Ek **line department** ko di
> gayi. Aur us department ki files mein kya hota hai? **Police firing ke inquiry reports, custodial
> deaths, police torture ke allegations, communal aur caste clashes, political aur student
> agitations, preventive detention — NSA aur COFEPOSA ke cases, aur Madras High Court aur Supreme
> Court mein habeas corpus petitions.** Yaani exactly woh files jinpar accountability sabse zyada
> zaroori hoti hai.
>
> Reaction tez tha. CPI(M) ke MP **S. Venkatesan**, DMK ke spokesperson **Saravanan**, Congress MP
> **Karti Chidambaram**, aur transparency groups — **Arappor Iyakkam** ke Jayaram Venkatesan aur
> **People's Watch** ke Henry Tiphagne. Government ki taraf se ministers **C.T.R. Nirmal Kumar** aur
> **Raj Mohan** ne defend kiya — Nirmal Kumar ka kehna tha ki exemption department ke sirf kuch
> sections par lagu hoti, poore department par nahi, aur ki community clash ki information sabse zyada
> maangi jaati hai.
>
> **27 September 2026** ko — chhah din baad — order **revoke** kar diya gaya. Naya order bas itna
> kehta hai: *"GO Ms. No. 57 … stands revoked."* Government ne publicly yeh nahi bataya ki order
> pehle kyun liya gaya tha.
>
> Do technical points, kyunki yahi exam-relevant hain.
>
> **Pehla:** exemption **absolute nahi hoti**. Section 24 ke tehat bhi, **corruption ke allegations
> aur human-rights violations** se related information ko exclude nahi kiya ja sakta. Human-rights
> violations wali information ke liye Act mein additional condition hai — aisi information State
> Human Rights Commission ke through, uski approval se, di jaati hai. Toh even agar woh order rehta,
> custodial death ya torture se judi information poori tarah band nahi hoti — lekin rasta lamba aur
> conditional ho jaata.
>
> **Doosra:** Section 24(4) mein **koi judicial pre-check nahi hai**. State government notification
> jaari karti hai, gazette mein chhapta hai, aur exemption lagu. Rokne ka practical tareeka **politics
> aur litigation** hai, koi automatic safeguard nahi. Isliye yeh episode ek warning hai: 24(4) ka
> khatra theoretical nahi, operational hai.

### ADD-36 — 7 August 2026: what the Supreme Court is actually asking
**Priority: P0** · **Runtime: ~2 min** · **Insert:** new block; **replaces** the "matter is pending" tail of `ADD-32`.

> DPDP ke through Section 8(1)(j) badal gayi — public interest override hat gaya. Isko challenge kiya
> gaya. **7 August 2026** ko Supreme Court ne suna. Ab tak aapko yahi pata tha ki "matter pending
> hai". Yeh kaafi nahi — **Court kya poochh raha hai**, woh sun lijiye.
>
> Bench thi **Chief Justice Surya Kant**, **Justice Joymalya Bagchi** aur **Justice V. Mohana** ki.
> Justice Bagchi ne sawaal aise frame kiya:
>
> *"The question is whether there can be a blanket prohibition on disclosure of all digital personal
> information, or whether the legislation has to adopt a proportionate approach."*
>
> Yaani — kya Parliament **saari** digital personal information ke disclosure par ek **blanket
> prohibition** laga sakti hai? Ya use **proportionate** hona padega?
>
> Aur unhone constitutional test bhi naam se bataya: Court dekhegi ki digital personal data ko
> alag treat karne wali **classification** ka us objective ke saath — jise achieve karna hai — kya
> **"reasonable and intelligible nexus"** hai, aur kya **intelligible differentia** maujood hai.
> Yeh Article 14 ki reasonable-classification language hai. Iska matlab: challenge sirf "privacy vs
> transparency" ki philosophical behes nahi hai — woh ek **classification aur proportionality**
> ka test hai.
>
> Court ne ek aur cheez kahi jo analytically important hai: **"RTI is a Central law. We need to
> actually harmonise these two laws."** Justice Bagchi ne kaha ki Court sirf data protection law
> nahi dekh rahi, uska RTI Act par asar bhi dekh rahi hai. Aur yeh bhi note hua ki RTI Act ka
> domain DPDP se **zyada chauda** hai — RTI har tarah ki information par lagta hai, DPDP sirf
> digital data par — chahe ab zyada-tar data digital hi ho.
>
> Petitioners ki taraf se **Prashant Bhushan** (NCPRI ke liye) ne kaha ki purani 8(1)(j) mein ek
> balance built-in tha, aur ab amendment **pending charge sheets against a public officer**, ya
> **welfare schemes aur ration cards** se judi details ko bhi sirf "personal information" kehkar
> roka ja sakta hai. **Nisha Bhambhani** ne investigative journalism ka point rakha — agar journalist
> ko report karne se pehle data principal ki consent leni pade, aur baad mein data principal erasure
> maang le, toh reporting effectively block ho jaati hai. **Vrinda Grover** ne kaha ki DPDP
> practically 8(1)(j) ko rewrite kar deta hai.
>
> Government ki taraf se **Solicitor General Tushar Mehta** ne kaha ki Union amendment ko apne
> affidavit mein justify karegi, judicial review ke subject. Unhone **do hafte** maange. Aur Court ne
> **Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology ko party banakar implead** kar diya.
>
> Ek procedural baat jo aapko pata honi chahiye: yeh matter pehle hi **February 2026** mein ek
> **larger — five-judge — bench** ke paas bheja ja chuka hai, aur Court ne amendment par **stay dene
> se mana** kar diya tha. CJI Surya Kant ne tab kaha tha: *"we might have to iron out some creases"*
> aur "personal information" ko define karna padega. Yaani **amended 8(1)(j) aaj poori taqat se
> lagu hai** — challenge pending hone ka matlab yeh nahi ki provision suspended hai.
>
> Case ka naam yaad rakhiye: **Venkatesh Nayak & Ors. v. Union of India**, jismein petitioners
> Venkatesh Nayak, **The Reporters Collective** aur journalist **Nitin Sethi**, **NCPRI**, aur
> journalist **Geeta Seshu** ke saath SFLC shaamil hain.

### ADD-41 — Section 7(9): what the law says, and what PIOs do with it
**Priority: P2** · **Runtime: ~55 s** · **Insert:** new block; **appended to** `ADD-23`.

> Section 7(9) par humne kaha tha: yeh **form** ke baare mein hai, exemption ke baare mein nahi. Yeh
> legally bilkul sahi hai. Ab practical caveat.
>
> Section 7(9) kehta hai ki information us form mein di ja sakti hai jis mein **maangi** gayi hai,
> **unless** woh us form mein dene se PIO ke resources par **disproportionate** burden pade, ya
> records ke preservation ko khatra ho. Yaani PIO information **dega** — bas format badal sakta hai.
>
> Practice mein iska istemaal aksar ulta hota hai. PIO "disproportionately divert resources" likhkar
> information **poori tarah deny** kar dete hain — jo 7(9) allow hi nahi karta. Multiple offices,
> multiple files, "bahut zyada documents" — yeh sab **exemption nahi**, **format** ka sawaal hain.
>
> Counter yeh hai: agar PIO 7(9) ke naam par deny kare, toh usse **do cheezein** likhwa lijiye —
> **(ek)** information kis form mein available **hai**, aur **(do)** us form mein dene se kaun sa
> resource disproportionate tarike se divert hoga. Aksar yahi do sawaal refusal ko tod dete hain. Aur
> agar denial phir bhi rahe, toh appeal mein saaf likhiye ki **7(9) ek exemption provision nahi hai**
> — Section 8 ka list exhaustive hai, aur 7(9) us list mein nahi hai.

### BONUS-20 — How to draft an application that survives
**Runtime: ~1 min 30 s** · **Insert:** immediately after `P04`.

> Ab practical hissa. Ek application jo PIO resistance survive kare, usmein **nau cheezein** honi
> chahiye.
>
> **Ek — PIO ka pura address block.** "To, The Public Information Officer", phir office ka pura naam,
> department, aur pura postal address. Galat address sabse common reason hai ki application pahunchti
> hi nahi. Agar PIO ka naam pata na ho, **Section 6(1)** ke tehat aapko naam jaanne ki zaroorat nahi
> — designation kaafi hai.
>
> **Do — subject line.** "Application under the Right to Information Act, 2005 — request for
> information regarding ___."
>
> **Teen — information sought.** Yeh sabse important hissa hai. Har sawaal ko **numbered point**
> banaiye. Aur **documents maangiye, opinion nahi.** "Aapne yeh decision kyun liya" — yeh information
> nahi hai, yeh opinion hai, aur PIO ise refuse kar dega. Uski jagah: "us decision se related file
> notings ki certified copies."
>
> **Chaar — period.** Har cheez ke liye **date range** dijiye — "1 January 2023 se 31 December 2025
> tak." Bina period ke "voluminous" bolkar refuse karna aasan ho jaata hai.
>
> **Paanch — citizenship declaration.** "I am a citizen of India." RTI ka right **Section 3** ke tehat
> **citizens** ko hai. Ek line, aur yeh argument band.
>
> **Chhe — fee details.** Fee kitni, kis mode se — IPO number, ya online receipt number. Aur agar BPL
> hain toh **Section 7(5)** ke under exemption ka hawala aur card ki copy.
>
> **Saat — applicant ka naam aur pura address.** Reply yahan aayega.
>
> **Aath — signature.** Aur date.
>
> **Nau — date of application.** Aur apni ek copy par **postal receipt** ya registration number
> sambhaal kar rakhiye. Yeh aapka proof hai ki application kab file hui — aur 30 din ki ghadi usi se
> chalegi.
>
> Do practical cheezein. **Pehli:** application **Speed Post** se bhejiye, registered post se nahi —
> delivery proof behtar milta hai. **Doosri:** agar information kisi **teesre paksh** se judi hai,
> toh PIO ko **Section 11** ke tehat notice dena padta hai, jisse 40 din lag sakte hain. Toh jab bhi
> ho, aise sawaal alag application mein bhejiye taaki baaki jawab block na ho.

### BONUS-21 — The four refusals you will actually meet, and the counter to each
**Runtime: ~1 min 40 s** · **Insert:** after `BONUS-20`.

> Section 8 ke exemptions theory mein padh liye. Practice mein refusal chaar shapes mein aata hai.
>
> **Ek — "Section 7(9): resources par disproportionate burden."** Jaisa humne `ADD-41` mein kaha —
> yeh **format** ka provision hai, exemption ka nahi. **Counter:** poochhiye information kis form mein
> available hai, aur kaun sa resource disproportionate tarike se divert hoga. Aur appeal mein likhiye
> ki **Section 8 exhaustive hai, 7(9) usmein nahi hai.**
>
> **Do — "Hum yeh record maintain hi nahi karte."** Yeh sabse common aur sabse slippery refusal hai.
> **Counter:** RTI Act PIO par **record create karne ka duty nahi** daalta — yeh bilkul sahi hai.
> Lekin **Section 2(f)** "information" ko bahut chauda define karta hai: documents, records, memos,
> emails, opinions, advices, press releases, circulars, orders, logbooks, contracts, reports, papers,
> samples, models, aur **kisi private body se access kiya gaya material**. Toh sawaal aise poochhiye
> jo **existing** record par pade — "file notings", "correspondence register", "inspection reports" —
> na ki aise jo nayi analysis maange. Aur agar record exist karta hai lekin "maintain nahi kiya
> jaata" kaha ja raha hai, toh woh **Section 4(1)(a)** ke record-management duty ka sawaal banta hai
> — jise complaint ke tehat uthaya ja sakta hai.
>
> **Teen — "Third-party information hai."** **Section 11** ka procedure: PIO ko request milne ke
> **5 din** ke andar third party ko written notice dena hota hai; third party ko **10 din** milte
> hain submission ke liye; aur PIO ko original request se **40 din** ke andar decision dena hota hai.
> Yeh **refusal nahi**, ek **process** hai. **Counter:** check kijiye ki notice diya bhi gaya ya nahi
> — bina notice ke Section 11 ka sahara lena galat hai. Aur **Section 11(1) ka proviso** dhyan se
> padhiye, kyunki yeh aksar ulta samjha jaata hai: *"**except** in the case of trade or commercial
> secrets protected by law, disclosure may be allowed if the public interest in disclosure outweighs
> in importance any possible harm or injury to the interests of such third party."* Yaani third-party
> information **public interest** ke naam par disclose **ho sakti hai** — **siwaye** un cases ke jahan
> woh trade ya commercial secret ho jo law se protected ho. Public-interest override hai, lekin uske
> andar ek exception hai.
>
> **Chaar — "Yeh vexatious ya frivolous hai."** RTI Act mein **kahin bhi "vexatious" ka zikr nahi
> hai.** Yeh ground statute mein maujood nahi. Kuch commissions ne apne orders mein aisi language
> use ki hai, lekin woh **statutory exemption nahi** hai. **Counter:** appeal mein seedha likhiye ki
> refusal kis **specific Section 8 clause** ke under hai — agar clause naam nahi liya gaya, toh
> refusal legally adhoora hai.
>
> Aur ek general rule jo sab par lagu hota hai: **Section 7(8)** ke tehat PIO ko refusal ka **reason
> dena zaroori hai**, saath mein woh period jismein appeal ho sakti hai, aur First Appellate Authority
> ka naam. Bina reason ka refusal — ya bina clause ka refusal — appeal mein sabse mazboot ground hai.

### BONUS-22 — First Appeal strategy: drafting to win
**Runtime: ~1 min 20 s** · **Insert:** after `BONUS-21`; cross-ref from `P12`, `BONUS-12`.

> First Appeal woh jagah hai jahan sabse zyada cases actually theek ho jaate hain — Commission tak
> pahunchne se pehle. Toh ise sahi likhna zaroori hai.
>
> **Timing:** **Section 19(1)** ke tehat PIO ke jawab se — ya 30 din khatam hone se — **30 din** ke
> andar. Late ho gayi toh Appellate Authority delay **condone** kar sakti hai, lekin "sufficient
> cause" dikhana padta hai.
>
> **Sabse zaroori line:** agar PIO ne **Section 8(1)** ke kisi clause par refuse kiya hai, toh appeal
> mein **Section 8(2) ko explicitly cite kijiye.** Section 8(2) ek **general override** hai — woh
> kehta hai: *"Notwithstanding anything in the Official Secrets Act, 1923 nor **any of the exemptions
> permissible in accordance with sub-section (1)**, a public authority may allow access to
> information, if public interest in disclosure outweighs the harm to the protected interests."*
>
> Dhyan dijiye — "**any** of the exemptions". Yaani Section 8(1) ke **kisi bhi** clause ke against
> yeh override available hai. Section 8(2) mein koi clause **carve-out** nahi kiya gaya hai. Yeh
> point exam aur practice, dono mein important hai, kyunki log aksar maante hain ki kuch clauses
> absolute hain — statute aisa nahi kehta.
>
> Aur isi wajah se yeh DPDP ke baad aur zyada zaroori ho gaya hai: amended **Section 8(1)(j)** ab
> sirf "information which relates to personal information" hai — uske andar ka public-interest
> override **hata diya gaya** hai. Toh ab personal information ke disclosure ka **eklauta statutory
> raasta Section 8(2) hai.** Agar aap appeal mein 8(2) nahi likhenge, toh Appellate Authority ke
> saamne balancing ka sawaal hi nahi aayega.
>
> Structure aise rakhiye:
>
> **Ek** — application ki date, registration number, aur PIO ke order ki date.
> **Do** — kya maanga tha, aur kya mila — ya kya nahi mila. Dono ka side-by-side.
> **Teen** — PIO ka reason, verbatim.
> **Chaar** — us reason ka point-wise jawab. Agar "vexatious" kaha gaya — likhiye ki Act mein yeh
> ground nahi hai. Agar "7(9)" kaha gaya — likhiye ki woh format ka provision hai. Agar 8(1) clause
> kaha gaya — **8(2) ka public-interest balance** maangiye.
> **Paanch** — relief clearly likhiye: information provide ki jaaye, penalty ke liye **Section 20(1)**
> ke tehat notice jaari ho, aur agar nuksan hua hai toh **Section 19(8)(b)** ke under compensation —
> with the loss spelled out.
> **Chhe** — saath mein PIO ka order, apni original application, aur fee proof attach kijiye.
>
> Ek aur practical tip: **Section 19(5)** ke tehat applicant ko **prove** karna nahi padta ki refusal
> galat tha — burden us par hai jisne refuse kiya. Appeal mein yeh line zaroor likhiye.
>
> Aur **fee** par dhyan dijiye: Central rules mein appeal ke liye koi fee nahi hai, aur CIC koi fee
> nahi leta. Lekin **kuch states leti hain** — Maharashtra ₹20 per appeal, Bihar ₹10. Punjab,
> Chhattisgarh aur Tamil Nadu mein appeal fee nahi. Apne state rules check kar lijiye.

### Also worth knowing before recording

- **`righttoinformation.wiki` is not a reliable source.** It is the origin of the "24 bodies" error
  (C5) and of several unattributable statistics in the review. Its CIC-backlog page cites
  "Section 19(7) BNSS 2024" — the BNSS is the criminal procedure code and has nothing to do with RTI
  appeals. Where I have used its numbers (CIC ~36,420 pending; the 7 Jul fee rollback; the 9-field
  application template) they are marked **secondary** in the register. Prefer the CIC's own MIS
  report and the state RTI portals.
- **The Maharashtra ₹20 appeal fee is not a residue of the 2026 rules.** ₹20 per appeal is the
  long-standing Maharashtra rate — the pre-2026 position was ₹10 application + ₹20 appeal, which the
  2026 rules took to ₹30 + ₹50/₹100, and the rollback restored. Framing it as "the ₹20 appeal fee
  remains" implies a new charge survived. It didn't.
- **The 2026 rules' commencement is reported two ways.** Economic Times says they "came into force
  immediately upon publication" (12 June); ThePrint says the changes "would be implemented from
  5 July". Both are quoted in the register. Safe line for air: *"notified on 12 June, and stayed
  before the 5 July implementation date that activists had marked for protest."*
